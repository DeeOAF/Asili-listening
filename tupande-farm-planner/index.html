How it works

Four steps, one conversation

The agent and farmer fill in the plan together, like a page in the agent's notebook. Every choice stays within what the farmer has.

  1. Your assets

    Trace the field's corners on the satellite map with GPS. Then enter the cash you can invest now and your year with Tupande.

  2. Family food and trees

    How many 90 kg bags of maize does your family need? Will you plant short rains? Do you already have mango or avocado trees?

  3. Choose your options

    Add extra maize, groundnuts, fruit trees, timber or chickens. Totals update as you go, and controls stop at your land and cash limits.

  4. Review and share

    Compare up to three alternatives, keep the plan you like, and send a picture and summary on WhatsApp.

Food security first

Your family's maize is protected before anything else.

Other options open only when both checks pass. If one fails, the planner shows the shortfall so you can correct your details. It never lowers your family's food needs for you.

Check 1

Is there enough land?

Family maize plus any existing fruit trees must fit inside the field. Each acre gives 20 bags per harvest.

10 bags ÷ 20 = 0.5 acres
or 0.25 acres if you also plant short rains

Check 2

Is there enough money?

Your cash plus your Tupande long-rains credit must cover the family-maize inputs. Once protected, this maize stays locked.

0.5 acres × KES 13,250 = KES 6,625

Be clear about estimates

What the planner leaves out

Results are an estimated return after listed costs, not a guaranteed profit. Prices, yields and survival rates are planning assumptions for this pilot, not buyer quotations or agronomic advice.

  • Labour, transport, harvesting and maintenance
  • Cash-flow timing and food stocks between harvests
  • Guaranteed funding for short-rains inputs
  • Poultry housing, feed or carrying capacity
  • Tree–crop shading and root competition
  • Future years' tree planting
  • Inflation, discounting or live market prices
  • Automatic field boundaries or land ownership checks
  • Orders, credit approval, payments or loans
  • Survey-grade measurement
Key planning assumptions
  • Short-rains maize uses the same area, yield and input cost. Its inputs are assumed to be paid later from long-rains proceeds, so they are not reserved upfront.
  • Space per mature tree: avocado 64.8 m², mango 80 m². Land is reserved for every seedling planted.
  • Existing fruit trees use their current age, with no survival discount.
  • Boundary timber is limited to one tree every 5 m of perimeter. Agent judgement is needed on shading.
  • Up to 100 chicks per batch and six batches a year are prototype limits, not farm advice.
  • Manual acreage assumes a square field to estimate the perimeter.
