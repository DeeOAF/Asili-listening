How it works

Four steps, one conversation

The agent and farmer work through the plan together. Every choice stays within what the farmer actually has.

Your assets

Use GPS and the satellite map to trace the field's corners. Then enter the cash you can invest now and your year with Tupande.

Family food and trees

Tell us how many 90 kg bags of maize your family needs, whether you plant short rains, and any mango or avocado trees you already have.

Choose your options

Add extra maize, groundnuts, fruit trees, timber or chickens. Totals update instantly and controls stop at your land and cash limits.

Review and share

Compare up to three alternatives, keep the plan you like, and share a picture and short summary on WhatsApp.

Food security first

Your family's maize is protected before anything else

Other options unlock only when both checks pass. If one fails, the planner shows the shortfall so you can correct your details. It never reduces your family's food needs for you.

Land check

Family maize plus existing fruit trees must fit inside the field. At 20 bags per acre, a family needing 10 bags reserves 0.5 acres for one long-rains harvest, or 0.25 acres if they also plant short rains.

Funding check

Your cash plus your Tupande long-rains credit must cover the family-maize inputs of KES 13,250 per acre. Once protected, this maize area stays locked.

What you can plan

Family maize, plus five ways to earn

Each option uses agreed planning assumptions for the Kakamega pilot.

Always included

Family maize

  • 20 bags per acre per season (90 kg bags)
  • Inputs KES 13,250 per acre: 5 seed bags, 1 DAP, 1 CAN
  • Food kept is valued at KES 3,000 a bag
Optional

Extra maize

  • Same yield and input cost as family maize
  • Extra bags are assumed sold at KES 3,000
  • Inputs can use your long-rains credit
Optional

Groundnuts

  • One quarter-acre package, long rains only
  • Inputs and financing KES 6,500, deducted at sale
  • 150 kg at KES 145/kg: KES 15,250 paid after deduction
Optional

Fruit trees

  • Avocado seedling KES 75, mango KES 20
  • First harvest in year 3; 70% of new seedlings assumed to survive
  • From year 5: KES 1,800 (avocado) or KES 2,000 (mango) a year per surviving tree
Optional

Grevillea timber

  • Boundary (5 m apart) or woodlot (3 × 3 m)
  • First 50 seedlings free, then KES 5 each
  • One-off sale from KES 1,500 a tree at year 5 to KES 5,000 at year 10
Optional

Chickens

  • Six-week-old chicks at KES 150 each
  • Sold from three months at KES 550–600; 10% mortality
  • Only the first batch is paid upfront; later batches come from sales

Be clear about estimates

What the planner does not include

Results are an estimated return after listed costs, not a guaranteed profit. Prices, yields and survival rates are planning assumptions for this pilot, not buyer quotations or agronomic recommendations.

  • Labour, transport, harvesting and maintenance costs
  • Detailed cash-flow timing or food stocks between harvests
  • Guaranteed funding for short-rains inputs
  • Poultry housing, feed or carrying capacity
  • Tree–crop shading and root competition
  • Future years' tree-planting cohorts
  • Inflation, discounting or live market prices
  • Automatic field boundaries or survey-grade measurement
  • Land ownership verification
  • Orders, credit approval, payments or loan processing
Key planning assumptions
  • Short-rains maize uses the same area, yield and input cost; its inputs are assumed to be paid later from long-rains proceeds, so they are not reserved upfront.
  • Mature-space allowance per tree: avocado 64.8 m², mango 80 m². Land is reserved for every seedling planted.
  • Existing fruit trees use their current age; no survival discount is applied to them.
  • Boundary timber is limited by the field perimeter, one tree every 5 m. Agent judgement is needed on shading.
  • The prototype allows up to 100 chicks per batch and up to six batches a year. These are prototype limits, not farm recommendations.
  • Manual acreage assumes a square field to estimate the perimeter.
